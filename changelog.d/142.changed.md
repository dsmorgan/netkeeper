Triage asks its question in the order you answer it, and stops guessing at the answer. The card is three steps now — the preferred name, then the tags, then "have you met them?" — with both editors on the card instead of hidden behind `p` and `t`, and the decision last, loudest, and in one place: Met, Not met and Skip are on the card and no longer repeated in the button row above it, which now leads with Name and Tag; an explainer above it says what the screen is for in the workflow's own words, and each answer carries its one-line definition beside the button that gives it. The whole decision row stays on screen without scrolling on a 1280x800 laptop, and no longer shifts when one contact's headline is longer than the last one's. None of the keys moved: `m`, `n`, `s`, `t` and `p` still work from anywhere, so a practised run costs what it always did. The batch that marked everyone with nothing on file as *not met* is gone: marking the people you have met is an affirmative pass, and a quiet message history is not evidence that two people never met. Batches from a tag you have given a meaning still decide either way, because that is your own rule about your own label, and `n` still marks one person not met by hand. Undo still takes back an automatic not-met batch already on your stack. Two smaller things with it: undo no longer drops keyboard focus to the page when it takes a row out of the queue list, and the triage card now carries `archived_at` and `merged_into_id`, so a client can tell "this contact left the queue" from "somebody edited a field" instead of reading it out of an error message.
