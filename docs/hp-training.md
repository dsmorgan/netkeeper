## Step 6: Organizing and Validating Your LinkedIn Network

With your personal website now part of your professional toolkit, it’s time to make the most of your existing network by organizing and validating your LinkedIn connections.

Why This Step Matters

### You’ve likely built a large LinkedIn network over the years—but how many of those people do you actually know? Step 6 helps you:

- Clean up and validate your contacts
- Focus your outreach on people you've truly connected with
- Prepare for more personalized and effective networking

### 1\. Export Your LinkedIn Connections

### Go to Settings & Privacy in LinkedIn → Data Privacy → Get a Copy of Your Data → Request only “Connections.”

- You’ll receive a download link within 24 hours (often sooner)
- Download the .zip file and extract it
- Open the connections.csv file using Excel, Google Sheets, or your preferred spreadsheet tool

**Tip:** If prompted to format the file, choose "Delimited" and select “Comma” as the separator.

### 2\. Identify People You’ve Met

### Insert a new column in the spreadsheet next to the LinkedIn URL column. Then:

- Go down the list and mark an X next to anyone you’ve met in person, via Zoom, or had a real conversation with.
- Don’t worry about their job title, employer, or how long it’s been—if you’ve connected even once, mark them.

Avoid overthinking this. Don’t assess whether they can help you right now. If you’ve met them, they’re part of your network.

### 3\. Sort and Save Your Validated List

- Sort the spreadsheet so all the Xs move to the top
- Delete the rows with no X
- Save this new list separately—this is your validated contact list
- Be sure to keep the original download as a backup

### 4\. Next Steps

### You’ll use this validated list in future steps to reconnect with your real network and expand your reach. For now:

- Focus only on identifying who you’ve met
- Don’t spend time tracking down emails or updating info yet—we’ll get there later

### 5\. Join a Contacts Training Event

### To get support and ideas on how to use your validated list, attend the Contacts Training sessions held on Wednesdays.

Reminder: Keep attending weekly networking events to refine your 60-second pitch and learn from others.

Once your validated contact list is ready, you’re set for **Step 7!**

## Step 7: Extracting Contact Information from Your Validated Network

With your validated LinkedIn contacts list in hand, Step 7 helps you enrich that list with email addresses, phone numbers, and current job information—either manually or using automation tools.

### What You’ll Need:

- Your validated list of LinkedIn connections with profile URLs
- Excel, Google Sheets, or your preferred spreadsheet tool
- Optionally: the LinkedHelper tool for data scraping (www.linkedhelper.com)

### Two Methods for Gathering Contact Information

### Option A: Manual Lookup

- Copy and paste each profile URL into your browser.
- Click the Contact Info link on their LinkedIn profile.
- Copy the email and (if available) phone number into your spreadsheet.

**Pros:**

- Helps you revisit past relationships
- Can prompt reconnection when they see you’ve viewed their profile

**Cons:**

- Time-consuming, especially with large networks

**Option B: Automated Scraping with LinkedHelper**

- Download and install [<ins>LinkedHelper</ins>](https://linkedhelper.com/) (Mac or PC)
- Log in to your LinkedIn account through the app
- Watch the overview video to configure settings properly
- Import up to 100 LinkedIn URLs at a time from your validated list
- Run the scraper and wait for it to collect:
    - Email address (often personal)
    - Mobile number (if available)
    - Location, company, and title

**Important:** Limit scrapes to 100 profiles at a time to avoid being flagged by LinkedIn.

When finished, LinkedHelper will generate a CSV file.

Cleaning and Formatting the Data

Once you have your CSV file from Linked Helper:

- Open it in Excel or Google Sheets
- Keep these essential columns:
    - LinkedIn Profile URL
    - Email Address
    - First Name
    - Last Name
    - Location
    - Current Company
    - Current Position
    - Phone Number (if available)
- Delete all unnecessary columns to clean your sheet.

Pro Tip: Save your cleaned file as a CSV (comma-separated values) to prepare for import into Fellow in Step 8.

Don’t Overthink Whom to Include

### If you only have 110 contacts, great—include all of them. If you have 500+, work in batches of 100.

### Focus on speed and accuracy, not perfection. You’ll reconnect and refine later.

### Once you’ve finished your first batch and saved it as a CSV, you’re ready for Step 8: Importing and Reconnecting.

## Step 9: Handling Responses and Building Momentum

Once your first campaign is sent, it’s time to manage the responses, schedule conversations, and build valuable connections.

### 1\. Stay Proactive

- Don’t let your network drive the process—you follow up.
- Use responses as an opportunity to schedule calls (phone, Zoom, or Teams).
- Even if someone sends a generic “Nice to hear from you,” reply with something personal and suggest a quick catch-up.

### 2\. Use Your Tools

- Suggest a time and include your Calendly link (without making it feel impersonal).
- Use your calendar to set placeholders until the time is confirmed.
- Create a dedicated folder in your email (e.g., “First 100”) to store all campaign replies.

### 3\. Do Your Homework

### Before each call:

- Research the person’s company.
- Be ready to discuss what’s happening in their world.
- Lead with curiosity and a genuine desire to help—not your own goals.

If you're more experienced, be ready to offer consulting or advisory support. If you're early in your career, ask for guidance—not a job.

### 4\. Be Helpful and Memorable

- Offer advice if they ask how you’re approaching your networking.
- Share your personal website if they ask more about you.
- Focus on what matters to them first—opportunities often emerge from helping others.

### 5\. Track Your Outreach

- Keep notes on who responded and when.
- Use calendar reminders to follow up if someone doesn’t reply or if a future reconnection is planned.

### 6\. Prepare for the Follow-Up Campaign

- About one week after your first campaign, it’s time to send a follow-up to those who didn’t respond
- The average open rate is 65–70%, but the response rate target is 35%.
- The second email often gets more responses than the first.

Be humble—people are busy. Many will respond with apologies or enthusiasm.

### 7\. Continue Attending Weekly Networking Events

### These events offer real-time support, shared learning, and accountability. Bring your questions, share your wins, and stay consistent.

### Once you’ve handled your first responses and scheduled some conversations, you’re ready for the final phase: Step 10!

## Step 10: Running Your Follow-Up Campaigns

After your first campaign, it’s time to ensure you're following up effectively—this is where most of the value emerges.

### 1\. Understand Response Rates

- The typical response rate for the first email is 12–15%.
- If you’re under 10%, don’t worry—it’s common and correctable with a follow-up.

### 2\. Sort Your Contact List

- Open your original contact list.
- Remove those who responded to your first campaign.
- The remaining contacts become your follow-up list.

### 3\. Create and Send a Follow-Up Campaign

- Use the email template provided in your training resources—keep it short and warm.
- Reference the email you sent “last week” (or “a couple weeks ago” if delayed).
- Don’t overthink the copy—your name is what matters most.
- Schedule it for Tuesday–Thursday, about a week after the first email.

### 4\. Expect More Responses

- Many people who ignored the first message will apologize and respond to the follow-up.
- You’ll often get a higher response rate on the follow-up than on the first email.

Tip: Always send yourself a test message and verify all links before scheduling.

### 5\. Continue Weekly Campaigns

### Every week, you should either:

- Send a follow-up to a previous campaign, or
- Launch a new initial campaign to the next batch of 100 people

You can overlap campaigns, but don’t let response management slip. Only scale as fast as you can personally handle follow-ups.

### 6\. Keep Scraping and Organizing

- Continue enriching your LinkedIn list with contact info using LinkedHelper.S
- crape and clean up to 100 new profiles per day.
- Keep growing your network and preparing for future campaigns.

### 7\. Adjust Based on Results

- If your follow-up response is low, consider sending individual emails instead of bulk.
- Track who replies, how they respond, and what approaches get the best results.

### 8\. Attend Weekly Networking Events

### These sessions help you adapt, troubleshoot, and learn from other members' experiences. Bring questions, share progress, and get inspired.

### You're now running a full networking system. By staying consistent, being thoughtful in your communication, and engaging with your network proactively, you’re building something powerful.
