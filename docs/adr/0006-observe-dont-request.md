# 0006. Observe, don't request

Date: 2026-09-24

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Spec 9.3 said netkeeper reads LinkedIn through its internal Voyager API, from inside the page: an in-page `fetch` to `/voyager/api/...` with the session's cookies and a `csrf-token` header. The first supervised sync (#31) sent one such request, got an answer without `elements`, and the DOM fallback then found nothing. The capture that followed (#149, 2026-09-24) explained why:

- The connections list and profiles no longer use Voyager. They use `flagship-web`, a React Server Components client. The connections page carries its first ten cards in its own HTML, and as a person scrolls, the page itself sends `POST /flagship-web/rsc-action/actions/pagination` and receives the next ten. The capture has no `relationships/dash/connections` call at all. Profiles load as `POST /flagship-web/in/<slug>/`, and the contact details arrive in `POST /flagship-web/rsc-action/actions/navigation` after a person clicks **Contact info**.
- Bot detection runs on these pages: PerimeterX sensors, reCAPTCHA Enterprise, and obfuscated telemetry posts. A request the page did not make is exactly what those look for: an in-page `fetch` of an endpoint the page no longer calls, with a header set the real client does not send, at a moment no scroll explains.
- The real client paces itself. In the capture, pagination answers arrive a quarter to half a second after the request, and the requests come several seconds apart, as the reading scroll allows. netkeeper's own guesses at that pacing (#149, item 7) are unnecessary once the page decides when to ask.

## Decision

netkeeper drives the page and reads what the page loads. It navigates its own tab, scrolls it with `BrowserRun.scroll`'s wheel replay, and reads the responses to requests the page itself sent on that tab. That reading is passive and read-only:

- **No interception.** No `route`, no `unroute`, nothing that holds a request, changes it, answers it, or cancels it (`continue_`, `fulfill`, `abort`), no header overrides. A response listener sees an answer after the browser already has it.
- **No requests of netkeeper's own.** No in-page `fetch`, and no Playwright API request context. Every request LinkedIn sees on netkeeper's tab is one its own page decided to send.
- **The seam is one method.** `BrowserRun.observe(match)` listens to the tab's `response` events and keeps the bodies of the responses whose method, origin, and path the match names, in arrival order, with bounded memory and a body timeout. It also listens, just as passively, to the tab's `requestfinished` and `requestfailed` events, only to say in fixed words how a request whose body could not be read ended (#200). `PageLike` stays narrow; the listener methods are borrowed by that one method. Bodies never reach a log.
- **Pages are parsed whole or not at all.** A flight payload the parser does not recognize is `RouteChanged`. A page of connections is handed on only when every card on it parsed, so a shape change can stop a run but can never write part of a page or put one person's details on another's URN.
- **Completeness is proven by the page.** The end of the connections list is an answer the page received that says so (no cards, a short page that asks for no next one, or a full page that asks for none once the run has seen as many people as the page's stated total), and a full sync is complete only when it also saw at least as many people as that total. A page that stops loading proves nothing.
- **Walls are judged by where the tab is, never by what a page links to.** Every logged-in page links to sign-out and sign-in paths, so the HTML of a document, or of an error answer, is never searched for wall paths. A wall served in place at the connections url, with no first screen, stops the run as `RouteChanged`: it flags nothing and raises no heat.

**One exception: the Contact info click.** The contact details only arrive when a person clicks **Contact info**; there is no page load that carries them. Enrichment may click that one control, once per profile visit, paced and budgeted as part of the visit (it spends no extra `profile_visits` unit and is covered by `contact_info_fetches`, which spec 9.6 already ties to visits). No other click is allowed. The click will be a narrow `BrowserRun` method of its own, added by the enrichment lane, and the modules that read the page's answers never click, type, or evaluate script themselves (`tests/test_browser_safety.py`). This amends ADR 0002's "scroll is the only automation" reading of spec 9.1 for that one control.

## Consequences

- LinkedIn sees netkeeper's tab behave like a person's: a page load, scrolling, and the page's own requests at the page's own pace. There is no request for bot detection to single out that a person's browser would not also send.
- netkeeper can only read what the page chooses to load. A field the page does not render is out of reach, and a page that changes when it loads data (an infinite scroll that becomes a "Show more" button) stops the sync as `RouteChanged` until the parser or the scroll is updated. That is the intended failure: a run stops and ages nobody.
- The first screen's total and the page's end-of-list answer are what make a full sync complete. If the total counts a member the list never shows, no full sync completes and nobody ages. That is the safe direction; the first supervised run checks it.
- Budgets still count units of work, not requests: one `connection_pages` unit reads about 40 connections, which the page loads in about four of its own answers.
- The in-page Voyager fetch is retired for connections. Enrichment still uses it until the enrichment lane moves profiles and contact info onto the same seam, which is also when the Contact info click is added.
- P2-08's DOM reader for the connections list is removed, along with `FallbackConnectionsSource`: its selectors were authored, never seen on the live page, and found nothing on the first supervised run. Reading the page's own answers replaces it rather than falling back to it. The contact-info half of `dom.py` stays, unwired, until the enrichment lane decides its fate.
- A future contributor keeps three things true: nothing routes or alters a request; nothing sends one netkeeper wrote; and the only input netkeeper gives a LinkedIn page is navigation, the scroll replay (which rests the pointer over the content before it, #192), and the one Contact info click (#190), which Playwright aims at the control's own box -- never at a pointer position nothing placed.

## Amendments

- 2026-09-24 (#192, as built): the first supervised connections sync built on this ADR never scrolled at all. Playwright's `mouse.wheel` fires at the virtual pointer's position, which starts at `(0, 0)` and stays there until something moves it; on flagship-web `(0, 0)` sits under the fixed global nav, not over the connections list, which scrolls inside its own container, not the document. A wheel replay that never moved the pointer scrolled the nav, which has nowhere to scroll to, so nothing happened -- the run's six idle scrolls proved the list's end without ever having read past the first screen, and it stopped safely as `RouteChanged`, ageing nobody. `BrowserRun.scroll` now moves the pointer to rest over the page's content once per tab (`BrowserRun._rest_pointer_over_content`, cleared and repeated whenever the tab is reopened) before that first wheel event: a few short `mouse.move` hops with small jitter, paced like a hand coming to rest.
  Where that rest point *is* mattered on review: a first cut aimed at a fraction of a guessed viewport size regardless of the tab's real window, and missed at several real widths a maximized window or a narrower one both produce. It now reads the on-screen box of the page's main content landmark (`Locator.bounding_box()` on `main`, the module-level `_content_box`) and rests near its top, jittered, falling back to a viewport guess only when there is no box to read. `bounding_box` is a read, not an input: Playwright resolves the locator in its own isolated utility world and reads the box over CDP, never by running script in the page's own execution context the way `evaluate` does, so page script can't see or answer it -- nothing about it resembles the `fetch` a page's bot-detection telemetry watches for, and it needs no exception to this ADR's read-only reading any more than the pointer move itself does.
  A resting hand lands wherever the content actually is -- most likely on a card, not blank space -- and this decision does not claim nothing reacts to that: a hover-triggered request the *page's own script* decides to send on its own account is exactly the kind of traffic this ADR already allows (the seam reads what the page sends, at its own pace; nothing here sends, routes, or alters a request either way). It is not a click, and not a hover resolved against a particular element the way `locator.hover()` would be -- a bare point is all `mouse.move` ever targets -- so, like the pointer move itself, it needs no exception to spec 9.1's "scroll is the only automation" the way the Contact info click below does.
  `tests/test_browser_safety.py` pins that `mouse.move` may appear only inside that one method -- scoped to the enclosing method, not merely the file, so a call relocated to a sibling method or dropped a level up into `scroll` itself still fails the check even though the file it lives in did not change. The smoke replica (`tests/smoke/test_observe_smoke.py`) was rebuilt to match a plausible real layout -- a fixed header across the top over a centered content column, `html`/`body` not scrolling at all -- so this class of bug is caught here again; against the old replica (document-level scrolling) it was invisible, which is why no test had caught it.
- 2026-09-24 (#192 review round 2, N1): the box-based target above still centered vertically on the box, which is right for a box whose height is the viewport's own sliver of the content, but wrong for one whose height is the *whole list* -- an in-flow `main` inside a scrolling ancestor (rather than scrolling itself) reports exactly that, and it grows as more pages load. Centering on a ~2300px box aimed the pointer hundreds of pixels below any real window, reproducing the original #192 symptom by a different route. The target now stays within a fixed span of the box's top instead (never past its true vertical center for a short box), the jitter bound is capped the same way and additionally by the tab's own known viewport height when that is known, and the horizontal target and jitter are held on-screen for a box that starts off it. The smoke replica now tests both plausible layouts -- `main` scrolling itself, and `main` as a plain in-flow element inside a scrolling `#scroll` ancestor -- at several window sizes; the second reproduces N1 on the pre-fix code and passes after.
- 2026-09-24, #190: Enrichment moved onto the same seam, and the in-page Voyager fetch (`linkedin/fetch.py`) and the unwired contact-info DOM reader (`linkedin/dom.py`, `linkedin/contact_info.py`) are removed. The click is `BrowserRun.click_contact_info`: it finds the control by its accessible role and name, refuses unless it is the only one and its `href` is the profile's `overlay/contact-info/`, pauses, and clicks once; the page is first scrolled back to the top with the wheel, so the link is on screen the way a person finds it. - 2026-09-25, #200: **one read-only CDP session per observed connections tab, for the body tap** (widened to enrichment's visits by #203, below). Supervised runs lost about one pagination answer in 5 to 10: `response.body()` failed although the page had the data and moved on. On an isolated Chrome, the one reproduction that gives exactly that is a streamed answer the page's own client aborts after reading it (`AbortController.abort()` or `reader.cancel()` while the stream is still open). Chrome then answers `Network.getResponseBody` with "No data found for resource with given identifier", `requestfailed` fires with `net::ERR_ABORTED`, and 40 of 40 such answers were lost. Service workers, late reads, reads in flight, keepalive, and workers lost nothing. Buffer pressure lost answers as "evicted", a different message. Larger buffers recovered none of the aborted answers, and reading on `requestfinished` is no earlier than Playwright's own read, because that event never fires for them. `Network.streamResourceContent` recovered all of them, byte for byte.
  `BrowserRun.observe(match, tap=True)`, which `PageConnections` uses (and, since #203, `PageProfiles` with a narrower match), therefore opens one CDP session on the run's own tab (`BrowserRun._open_body_tap`). It was the only `new_cdp_session` in the package until #444 added the Message click's geometry read (below), and that session sends exactly two methods:
  - `Network.enable`, with bounded buffers of its own and no other option (32 MB total, 8 MB per answer). The session hears the tab's network events. It changes no request and nothing the page can see; Playwright's own session already sends the same command.
  - `Network.streamResourceContent`, for an answer the tap's match names, when its response arrives. Chrome then forwards that answer's data to this session as it arrives. Chrome refuses the call for an answer that has already finished, and the page's own copy of such an answer reads normally.

  Neither method holds, changes, blocks, answers, or adds a request, and the delay either adds is negligible. Chromium's `inspector_network_agent.cc` gives each DevTools session its own network agent: this session's `Network.enable` and buffers are its own, not Playwright's, and streaming only copies data the renderer already received to this session, with no backpressure on the page's loader. On the isolated Chrome, with the session on and off, the server received identical requests (count, method, path, body, and headers), and the page's timing stayed within run-to-run noise (650 ms off, 629 to 670 ms on).
  The streamed data is decoded: `Network.dataReceived` carries the bytes after content decoding. The #202 review ran a gzip-encoded variant of the abort-after-read smoke test on Chrome 154, and the copy parsed and stood in.
  The streamed copy is a second copy, never the body. `Observation` hands it over only when its own read failed (`ObservedResponse.streamed`), and `PageConnections` uses it only when it parses whole and agrees with what the page did next:
  - When the page asked for a next start, the copy must ask for that same start and hold exactly the cards in between.
  - When the page went quiet, the copy must prove the end of the list.

  - A failed stream counts only when Chrome marks it `canceled` with `net::ERR_ABORTED`, the page's own cancel. Any other failure may have cut it short.

  Anything else leaves the answer lost, under #200's rules. The rule for a quiet page rests on the parser's strictness: a copy cut short does not parse, so it cannot pass for a short last answer. The run must also account for every place the first screen's total names before a copy proves the end. `tests/test_browser_safety.py` allows `new_cdp_session` at that one site only (`ALLOWED_CONTEXT_MUTATIONS`), and allows `send` only inside that function, only with those two methods named as literals (`READ_ONLY_CDP_METHODS`, `CDP_SENDERS`). A future method, even a read-only one, needs its own entry and its own line here. A browser that can't open the session, or doesn't know either method, reads the way it did before.
- 2026-09-25, #203: **the body tap also serves enrichment's lazy cards and Contact info overlay.** The first live enrichment lost lazy cards the same way #200 lost pagination answers: `requestfailed` fired with `aborted`, and `response.body()` found no data. Both answers are streamed flight actions (`actions/component` and `actions/navigation`) that the page's own client can abort after reading them, which Chrome reports as `net::ERR_ABORTED`. `PageProfiles.open_profile` now observes each visit with the same tap, through the same `BrowserRun._open_body_tap`, sending the same two methods and nothing else. Its tap's match is narrower than the visit's observation: it names only the lazy cards' `POST .../actions/component` and the overlay's `POST .../actions/navigation`, never the profile's document or screen. `BrowserRun.observe` refuses a tap match that isn't a narrowing of the observation's (another origin, or a rule the observation doesn't have), before any session opens.
  A streamed copy stands in for a lost enrichment answer only under #200's rules, and only when it is whole by its own structure. The tap hands over a copy only when Chrome finished the stream, or it failed `canceled` with `net::ERR_ABORTED`; any other failure may have cut it short. That cancel does not prove the page read the whole answer: the page's own client cancelling after it read everything gives it, and so does a cancel mid-stream or a navigation away. So the check never rests on it. The copy must fit the body limit and parse as flight, and `flight.is_whole` must hold (#207 review). Row `0`, the root, must be there. Every id-bearing reference reachable from it (`$<id>`, and `$` with one of `L`, `@`, `Q`, `W`, `K`, `B`, `F`, `R`, `r`, `X`, `x`, `h` before the id, with or without a `:path`) must name a row, an import, or a text row the copy holds. Every model row must be reachable from the root. Rows arrive in no order a reader can rely on. React's `outlineModel` writes a child before the row that refers to it, and our fixtures and the #149 capture put the root after its children, so a cut copy may lack the root, or may hold the root and name a row it never got. Both fail. On the capture's two lazy cards and its overlay, no proper row-boundary prefix passes, and every whole answer does. The copy is then read exactly as strictly as a body: a lazy card must name no other member, and an overlay must name this profile and answer the page's own request for it. A copy that fails any of these leaves the answer lost under #197's rules: a lazy card is skipped, and an overlay makes the visit unreadable with no second click (superseded by the #405 entry below: a lost overlay now saves the profile without contact info and counts toward no unreadable limit; there is still no second click). Unlike the connections list, nothing the page asks for next confirms a lazy card or an overlay, so the structure is all there is. One more guard sits in the core: a harvest whose Contact info came from a copy says so (`ProfileHarvest.contact_info_from_copy`), and one that holds no email and no phone is written but leaves the contact due (`crm/apply.py` does not set `last_enriched_at` or clear `enrich_priority`), so a copy cut short in a way the structure did not catch costs a revisit, not a person's details. The run notes each visit read from a copy.
  `tests/test_browser_safety.py` pins the two observations that may open a tap (`TAP_OBSERVERS`: `PageConnections._land` and `PageProfiles.open_profile`), each at one call. It refuses a `tap` passed anywhere else, including one in `**kwargs`, and any reach of `observe` that is not a plain call (a held method, `getattr`, `functools.partial`, `operator.methodcaller`), since its keywords can't be read off the line. `_open_body_tap` may be reached only from `BrowserRun.observe` (`TAP_OPENER`). The CDP rules (`ALLOWED_CONTEXT_MUTATIONS`, `CDP_SENDERS`, `READ_ONLY_CDP_METHODS`) are unchanged.

- 2026-10-07, #195: **the run's tab opens in the background.** `context.new_page()` opens a foreground tab, and on macOS Chrome then activates its window and takes keyboard focus from the app you're using. `BrowserRun._open_tab` opens a browser-level session instead and sends `Target.createTarget` with only `url` (`about:blank`) and `background: true`, the call `new_page()` makes, minus the foreground. It finds the new tab by its target id. While it waits (at most 5 seconds), `BrowserRun._target_id` attaches a page-level session to every tab that appears in the context, including one you open or a popup a page opens in that moment, sends that tab `Target.getTargetInfo` with no params, and detaches. That's a read: it doesn't navigate, close, or give input to any tab, and the page can't see it. Only the tab whose target id matches is taken, so a tab you open at the same moment is never the run's. A created tab that never shows up as a page, or a wait cancelled before the match, is closed by that target id and no other (`BrowserRun._close_unclaimed_tab`, `Target.closeTarget`). If the background open fails, the run opens its tab with `new_page()` as before and its notes say Chrome may have taken focus. Once a prefill or an auto-send has brought its tab forward, a tab it reopens opens in front with `new_page()`, as before #195, so its inputs never land in a tab behind the one you see. `Page.bringToFront` and `Target.activateTarget` are never sent; the one `bring_to_front` stays the prefill's (ADR 0007). On an isolated Chrome, a background tab under Playwright still reports `visible` (Playwright turns on focus emulation for every tab it attaches to), runs `requestAnimationFrame` at about 30 frames a second, fires `IntersectionObserver` on a wheel scroll, and takes a Playwright click. `tests/test_browser_safety.py` pins the two new sites and their methods (`TAB_CDP_METHODS`).
